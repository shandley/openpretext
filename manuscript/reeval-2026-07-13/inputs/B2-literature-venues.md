## Part 1 — Current genome-curation practice and the frontier

### How the big projects curate today

The dominant workflow across Darwin Tree of Life (DToL), the Vertebrate Genomes Project (VGP),
and the wider Earth BioGenome Project is the Sanger Genome Reference Informatics Team (GRIT)
"Rapid Curation" pipeline. The pattern is: automated scaffolding produces a draft, then a human
curator inspects a Hi-C contact map and makes evidence-driven edits.

- Assemblies are scaffolded with Hi-C, then a curator inspects the contact map and corrects missed
  joins, mis-joins, contamination, and duplicate (haplotig) sequence. Duplicates are tagged and
  removed. [SEARCH — DToL genome-note methods text, e.g. https://wellcomeopenresearch.org/articles/11-380]
- Flat files and the maps used in curation are generated by the Sanger **TreeVal** pipeline;
  manual curation is done primarily in **PretextView**, with extra context from **JBrowse2** and
  **HiGlass**. **PretextSnapshot** renders the final contact map. [SEARCH — same DToL methods text]
- The Rapid Curation code is public at https://gitlab.com/wtsi-grit/rapid-curation. GRIT curates
  for the Genome Reference Consortium, VGP, and DToL. [SEARCH — same source]
- The foundational method paper is Howe, Chow, Collins, Pelan, Pointon, Sims, Torrance, Tracey,
  Wood, "Significantly improving the quality of genome assemblies through curation," *GigaScience*
  10(1):giaa153, 2021, DOI 10.1093/gigascience/giaa153. [FETCHED — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7794651/]
  Curators use the gEVAL database/visualization and, where available, Hi-C maps to check each
  discordance and decide whether to break, join, or reorder/reorient sequence. Across 111 assemblies
  totalling 174 Gb, curation applied on average 221 interventions per Gb. That number is the best
  single quantitative statement of how much manual work curation still is. [FETCHED — same PMC page]

The training side of this practice is real and citable: the Biodiversity Genomics Academy (BGA)
and EBP-Nor run hands-on "manual curation with Hi-C and JBrowse" courses, and there are Galaxy /
WorkflowHub workflows for "Hi-C contact map for assembly manual curation."
[SEARCH — https://github.com/BGAcademy23/manual-curation ;
https://github.com/ebp-nor/genome-assembly-workshop-2023/blob/main/11_PretextView.md ;
https://workflowhub.eu/workflows/1327]

### Where the Hi-C scaffolders sit (the step before curation)

Curation tools operate downstream of a Hi-C scaffolder. Names verified this session:

- **YaHS** ("yet another Hi-C scaffolding tool"), Zhou, McCarthy, Durbin, *Bioinformatics*
  39(1):btac808, published Dec 2022 / Jan 2023. Reported higher contiguity and accuracy than
  SALSA2 and pin_hic on the same data. Repo: https://github.com/c-zhou/yahs.
  [SEARCH — https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/39/1/btac808/6917071 and
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9848053/]
- **SALSA2** and **pin_hic** are the prior Hi-C scaffolders it is benchmarked against. [SEARCH — same]
- Independent benchmarking of Hi-C scaffolders (2023–2024) exists, e.g. a *Frontiers in
  Bioinformatics* 2024 plant-genome benchmark. [SEARCH —
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioinformatics/articles/10.3389/fbinf.2024.1462923/full]

Placement of OpenPretext: after the scaffolder (YaHS/SALSA2) and in the same slot as PretextView —
the human QC-and-correct step, not the scaffolding algorithm itself.

### The incumbent curation/visualization tools (what OpenPretext competes with)

- **PretextView** — Sanger desktop app for viewing `.pretext` contact maps; the de-facto standard
  curation UI in the DToL/VGP pipeline. Desktop, not web. [SEARCH — Howe 2021 and Arima tool blog
  https://discovery.arimagenomics.com/resources/blog/tools-for-genome-assemblies/]
- **Juicebox / Juicebox Assembly Tools (JBAT)** — desktop Java; JBAT allows visualization and
  manual curation of scaffolded assemblies. Repo https://github.com/aidenlab/Juicebox. [SEARCH — Arima blog]
- **HiGlass** — web-based, but built for *exploring* interaction maps, not for making and exporting
  curation edits (Kerpedjiev et al. 2018). [SEARCH — Arima blog / 4DN software list]
- **HiCExplorer** — processing/normalization/visualization suite, not a curation editor. [SEARCH — same]

So the genuinely web-native, curation-editing niche is not currently occupied by a mainstream tool:
HiGlass is web but exploratory; PretextView and JBAT are the editors but are desktop. This is the
clearest "gap in the market" argument for OpenPretext, and it is defensible from the sources above.

### The frontier and what is unsolved

1. **Curation is still a manual expert bottleneck.** 221 interventions/Gb (Howe 2021) is the
   quantitative statement of the problem. Every large project is time-limited by expert curator
   availability. [FETCHED — PMC7794651]
2. **Automation of curation is the active research front, and it is not solved.** The leading
   automated attempt is **AutoHiC**, a deep-learning method for automatic chromosome-level assembly,
   published in *Nucleic Acids Research* 52(19):e92, 28 Oct 2024 (preprint Aug 2023). Trained on
   ~500k Hi-C images from ~300 species; reported average error-detection accuracy >90%. The authors
   frame it as *complementing*, not replacing, manual curation. [SEARCH —
   https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/19/e92/7759145 ;
   https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.08.27.555031v1.full]
3. **Specialized hard cases remain manual.** e.g. bird microchromosomes: **MicroFinder** (bioRxiv
   2025) is a conserved-gene-set mapping aid for manual curation of microchromosomes, i.e. a new
   assist tool precisely because automation fails there. [SEARCH —
   https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.09.653066]
4. **Collinearity/synteny-driven correction GUIs are appearing** (e.g. a Collinearity-based
   Assembly Correction Tool GUI, *G3* 2025), showing continued demand for interactive correction UIs.
   [SEARCH — https://academic.oup.com/g3journal/article/15/2/jkae277/7906735]
5. **Reproducibility and training of curation are under-served.** Curation decisions are expert
   judgement, hard to reproduce or teach; the community response so far is courses and workflow
   packaging (BGA, EBP-Nor, Galaxy/WorkflowHub) rather than tooling that records and replays decisions.
   [SEARCH — BGA / EBP-Nor / WorkflowHub links above]

Net: the frontier is (a) push automation further (AutoHiC-style) and (b) make the human step faster,
more accessible, more reproducible, and teachable. OpenPretext's web-native + reproducible-log +
teaching-curriculum angle sits in (b), which is comparatively empty of mainstream competitors.

## Honest notes on fit — web-native curation tool vs incumbents

- **The strongest positioning fact is verifiable:** the mainstream curation *editors* (PretextView,
  Juicebox/JBAT) are desktop, and the mainstream *web* tool (HiGlass) is exploratory, not a curation
  editor. A browser-native curation editor that reads native `.pretext` and exports AGP/FASTA is a
  real, defensible niche. Sources: Arima tool blog + Howe 2021.

- **Best tool-paper fit is a tie between Bioinformatics Application Notes and NAR Web Server.**
  - Bioinformatics App Note: cleanest fit for "novel software / web interface," well understood by
    the genomics-tools audience, tight ~2,600-word format. Requires freely available, no mandatory
    registration — OpenPretext satisfies both. Fastest, most conventional route.
  - NAR Web Server: higher-profile venue for a genuinely web-hosted tool, but it demands a
    pre-approval proposal, a fully functional server that computes on *user-submitted* data, and a
    cover letter listing competitors. OpenPretext runs client-side in the browser (no server-side
    computation on uploaded data); the "computations on user-submitted data" criterion is worded
    around server-side compute, so confirm with the editor that a client-side WebGL tool qualifies
    before investing in a proposal. [SEARCH — NAR web server submission page]

- **GigaByte is the best "tool + reproducibility + data" fit.** Its FAIR / reproducibility / linked
  code-and-data model and Technical Release type match a scientific-correctness-first curation tool,
  and its updatable-article model suits a living tool + curriculum. Lower profile than NAR but
  strongly aligned in values. [FETCHED]

- **JOSS is a clean secondary/companion paper**, not a primary claim venue. It reviews the software
  itself openly on GitHub and gives a citable DOI, but the "paper" is a ~1-page wrapper and it wants
  evidence of research impact/adoption. Good to pair with a fuller App Note, weak as the sole output.
  Watch the "feature-complete, not a thin client, sustained open development" bar. [FETCHED]

- **BMC Bioinformatics and F1000 are solid fallbacks.** BMC Software articles fit and have a clear
  metadata checklist; F1000's Software Tool Article explicitly names "web tools" and workflows, and
  its open post-publication review + fast versioned publishing suits an iterating tool, at the cost
  of prestige perception. [SEARCH / FETCHED respectively]

- **For a comparative-genomics BIOLOGY paper (the P(s)-decay / cross-species finding), Genome
  Research and Genome Biology are the targets, not the tool venues.** Caveat straight from Genome
  Research's own current guidance (via search summary): a genome/assembly contribution alone is no
  longer competitive and computational-only work without biological consequence is desk-rejected —
  the biological claim has to lead. Keep the tool paper and the biology paper separate; do not try
  to sell OpenPretext as the headline in a Genome Research/Genome Biology submission.

- **Education contribution:** PLOS Comp Biol Education (Ten Simple Rules / Quick Tips) fits a
  "how to curate a genome assembly" teaching piece, but it is short (≤2,500 words) and usually
  invited — query the editor first. GigaByte or F1000 can carry a tool-plus-curriculum piece without
  the invitation gate.
